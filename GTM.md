# Go-To-Market Strategy

## Exact Target User

**Primary:** Engineering Manager at a Series A–B SaaS startup, 15–80 employees, who approved AI tools reactively as developers requested them over the past 18 months and now has no consolidated view of what the team is paying. They're not the one paying the bills directly (finance is), but finance started asking questions last quarter and they don't have good answers. They feel mild anxiety about this and would act on a tool that gave them clarity in under 5 minutes.

**Job title:** Engineering Manager, VP of Engineering, CTO (at companies <50 people where the CTO still manages the stack).

**Company stage:** Series A or B. Pre-Series A teams are too small to have tool sprawl. Post-Series B finance teams have already solved this with procurement software.

**Secondary:** Senior developer at a startup who personally pays for 2–3 AI subscriptions on their own card and has never done the math on what they're spending or whether it's worth it.

## What They're Googling Right Before They Want This

- "cursor vs github copilot which is better 2026"
- "claude vs chatgpt for coding teams"
- "how much does github copilot cost for a team of 10"
- "ai tools budget for engineering team"
- "are we overpaying for ai subscriptions"

They're not searching for "AI spend audit tool" — they don't know that category exists yet. They're searching around the problem, not the solution.

## First 100 Users in 30 Days — $0 Budget

**Week 1: Show HN**
Post "Show HN: SpendLens — free AI spend auditor for dev teams, no login required" on a Tuesday or Wednesday morning (9am ET). The no-login angle is the hook — HN users are allergic to email gates. Aim for 50+ upvotes to hit the front page. This alone could drive 500–2,000 visitors. Target: 30 audits from HN.

**Week 1–2: Reddit**
Post in r/ExperiencedDevs: "I made a free tool to audit your team's AI tool spend — curious if others are seeing the same overlap patterns I found." Lead with curiosity, not promotion. Share a real example audit result (screenshot of a realistic 5-person team paying for Cursor + Copilot + ChatGPT). Target: 20 audits.

**Week 2: Cold DMs on X**
Find 50 engineering managers who have recently tweeted about AI tools, Cursor, or team productivity. Send a 2-sentence DM: "Saw your tweet about Cursor — built a free audit tool that shows what teams your size typically overpay for. No login, takes 2 minutes: [link]. Would love your feedback." Target: 10 audits, 3 replies with feedback.

**Week 2–3: Dev newsletters**
Reach out to Changelog, TLDR Tech, and JavaScript Weekly for a free listing or "tools we're watching" mention. These have 50k–200k subscribers each. One mention = 100–500 visitors. Target: 20 audits.

**Week 3–4: Share link virality**
Every audit generates a shareable URL. The results page is designed to be screenshot-worthy (big savings number, clean layout). Encourage sharing explicitly: "Share this with your manager." Each shared audit is a new distribution event. Target: 20 audits from organic shares.

## The Unfair Distribution Channel

Credex has a list of companies that have already purchased AI credits — they're proven AI spenders. A warm email from Credex to existing customers ("We built a free tool to audit whether you're getting the most out of your AI stack") has a conversion rate 10x higher than cold outreach. No other tool launching in this space has access to a pre-qualified list of AI buyers. This is the moat.
